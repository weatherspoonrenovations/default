Layout study · single-story, 3 bed / 1 bath

Master Suite & Great Room Plan

The rear room becomes the master suite, with the old laundry and closet turned into its bath. The wall between the front bedroom and the living room comes out to make one great room. The washer and dryer move to a closet at the end of the bedroom hall. You end up with 3 bedrooms and 2 baths, two of them primaries.

What changes

  1. 1

    Great room engineer

    Take out the 10'9" wall between the front bedroom and the living room. The two rooms together come to about 350 sq ft, and the hall now opens straight into it.

    That wall runs down the middle of the house, the long way, which is where a ranch's ceiling joists usually land. Assume it is bearing until the attic says otherwise. The usual fix is a flush LVL beam in the ceiling, carried on posts at each end down to footings, sized by an engineer. Build temporary shoring walls on both sides before it comes out.

    • The old bedroom's closet nook beside the kitchen can stay as a built-in or bar niche, or come out with the wall.
    • Expect to patch flooring and ceiling along the old wall line. Bedrooms and living rooms often have different floor finishes.
  2. 2

    Master bath, 6'2" × 7'9"

    Remove the wall between the rear room and the laundry, and the small closet in the room's corner. The two spaces become one bath: a 3' × 3' shower in the back corner, a toilet beside it, and a 48" vanity along the outside wall.

    This is the simplest place for a bath because water lines and a drain already sit there for the washer. A washer drain is 2", though, and a toilet needs 3", so run a new 3" line out to the main and vent it. Close up the old laundry door into the kitchen, and add an exhaust fan ducted outside.

  3. 3

    Closet and bath door

    A 6'4" reach-in closet backs onto the bath, with bypass doors facing the bedroom. The bath gets a pocket door so no swing eats into the room. A queen bed goes on the back wall under the window, with space for a dresser beside the closet.

  4. 4

    Suite door off the kitchen

    The suite is still entered through the kitchen, which keeps the plan simple. Put a pocket door in that opening so the master suite can be closed off.

  5. 5

    Laundry at the end of the hall

    The small closet at the end of the bedroom hall is turned to face the hall and gets bifold doors. A stacked 27" washer and dryer fits inside. This puts laundry next to the bedrooms, where the clothes are, and 4 to 7 feet from the hall bath's plumbing wall. The dryer vent runs through the neighboring closet to the outside wall, a run of about 4 feet.

    • Hot and cold supply with a washer box, a 2" standpipe with trap and vent, and a pan with a drain under the washer.
    • A 30 A, 240 V circuit for the dryer (or a gas line) and a 20 A circuit for the washer.
    • If that closet belonged to the front bedroom, the bedroom still has its corner closet, drawn at the lower right of that room.
  6. 6

    Open it all the way optional

    Tick "Also open the kitchen and dining walls" above the plan to see the walls between the great room, the kitchen and the dining area come out too. That makes kitchen, dining and living one room about 25' × 26'. These walls run across the house, so they are less likely to be bearing, but check the attic for joist splices sitting on them. Any kitchen cabinets on the old bedroom side of the kitchen wall would need to move.

Check before pricing. The great-room wall and the new toilet drain are the two items that set the budget. Look in the attic to see which way the joists run over the bedroom wall. Find out whether the rear room sits on a slab or a crawlspace, because that decides how hard the 3" drain is to run.

Both jobs need permits: structural for the beam, and plumbing and electrical for the bath and the laundry.
