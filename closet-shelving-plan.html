Toy Closet Build Plan

One wall-hung plywood unit runs the full 87 3/8" of the back wall in three bays: bins plus the generator at the left, five tiers of toy and supply bins in the middle, and a floor-to-ceiling utility bay at the right where the laundry chase makes the floor unusable anyway.

Every closet dimension below is yours, measured on site. The generator is 16" wide, 14" high and 13" deep, so it sits on a shelf rather than owning a floor bay.

Orientation throughout: you stand in the door opening facing into the closet. The back wall is the long wall in front of you. The front wall is the one the door is cut into, behind you. Left and right are your left and right.

What is on the other side

Two of the four walls are the back of something finished, which changes what you are allowed to fasten into.

  • The back wall carries the dining room's lit nook. What reads as a soffit from inside the closet is the back of a shallow illuminated display nook above the dining table. It is a light box with an LED channel in it, not framing.
  • The left end wall carries a recessed, lit bookcase. The nook on the other side has LED strips under its shelves.
  • The two boxes in the left wall pocket are the drivers for both. They take 120V and step it down to 12V: one feeds the shelf nook, one feeds the dining room nook. Low-voltage cable runs from that pocket to both, through the left wall and across into the back wall's nook.

Fasten nothing to the nook, ever. No cleat, no screw, no shelf bearing on its face or underside. There is an LED channel and its wiring inside it, its structure is unknown from this side, and it is finished work in another room. Everything in this plan stops below 76" for exactly that reason. The one option in earlier drafts that hung a shelf off it has been removed.

Scan the left wall before you drive a single 2 1/2" screw into it. The bookcase on the other side is recessed, so the partition between it and the closet may be much thinner than a normal stud wall, and it carries low-voltage LED cable that a live-wire detector will not see. Find out how thick that wall really is first. If it turns out to be a shallow cavity, do not fasten cleats to it: add a plywood end panel at the left end of bay A instead, per the options below. The back wall is ordinary below 76" and takes the unit's load normally.

What the shape dictates

At 36 1/2" deep this is a step-in closet, not a walk-in. The whole design follows from three consequences of that.

  • All the storage goes on the back wall. It is 87 3/8" of uninterrupted wall, which is more than the old plan got from three walls combined. Shelves 16" deep leave 20 1/2" of standing room, which is enough to step in, turn, and pull a bin with both hands.
  • The right end is a pocket. The laundry chase projects 22" and leaves a 14 7/8" slot in front of the back wall's right 17 1/8". Nothing wide gets in or out of there, so it becomes the broom-and-vacuum bay, where things stand on the floor and nothing needs reaching around.
  • The soffit sets the ceiling for two thirds of the wall. Its underside is at 76" and its right edge lands at 62 1/8". That edge becomes a structural division: the bays under it stop at 74 1/4", the bay past it runs to the ceiling.

The back wall unit

The two dividers are 3/4" plywood. The one at 27 1/2" runs floor to 75". The one at 62 1/8" runs floor to ceiling and lands exactly on the soffit's right edge, which is what lets bay C go full height without any notching.

Bay A runs on its own shelf lines. Shelf tops at 15, 32, 47, 62 and 74 1/4 rather than bay B's 15, 30, 45, 60 and 74 1/4. The 2" offset buys a 16 1/4" tier between 15 and 32 for the generator, which needs 14" plus room to lift it out and room for its fan. Every other bay A tier still clears 12 1/4" or more, which takes a tall bin. A 2" step at the divider reads as deliberate; a 1" step would read as a mistake, which is why it is 2".

The left end wall

This wall carries the LED drivers, the outlet, and the lit bookcase on its far side. It is the only place in the closet where something in the plan comes close to live equipment. The driver pocket sits high at the back corner and projects barely over an inch, so it is no obstacle at shelf height; the question is purely how low its bottom edge hangs.

Power

The outlet is on the left end wall, low, forward of the drivers, so the generator goes to the left end of bay A, hard against that wall, on the 15" shelf. Its top sits at 29", so the display reads at a glance and you lift it out with two hands at hip height instead of squatting to the floor. Its cord drops off the left end of the shelf and runs down the wall to the outlet, a run of a couple of feet at most. Notch the left-rear corner of that shelf about 1 1/2" square so the cord passes without being pinched against the cleat.

Leave the 16 1/4" tier open at the front, with no door and nothing stacked on top of the generator. These units run a fan while charging and while delivering, and 2 1/4" of headroom plus an open face is what keeps it breathing. The 11" of shelf left beside it, now on its right, takes one standard bin, or stand flat goods on edge there: cutting mats, clipboards, poster board.

Measure how far the outlet sits from the back corner before you build. The unit is 16" deep, so anything closer than 16" to the back wall ends up behind it. That is survivable for a generator you plug in once and leave, but you would not be able to unplug it without pulling a shelf. If it measures under 16", either move the rough-in forward to about 20", which is a drywall patch and nothing more, or accept the plug-once arrangement knowingly. Past 16" it stays in the open and there is nothing to do.

If your vacuum is a cordless stick that charges on a dock, add a second receptacle in bay C at 40" above the floor, above the vacuum's body and below the 72" shelf.

The LED drivers are on the left end wall, not the back wall. They sit high at the back corner in a pocket 15 1/4" wide by 14 3/8" tall that projects only about 1 1/4". Nothing in the main build reaches it: the top shelf lands at 74 1/4" and its left-wall cleat stops at 73 1/2".

Drivers are not junction boxes, and the difference matters here. They run warm and they are the component in that wall most likely to fail and need swapping, so the cover has to come off easily and the pocket has to breathe. Use a screwed-on painted panel with a vent: a row of holes behind a strip of perforated metal, or a slotted lower edge. Never caulk, tape or mud it shut, and never stack anything against it. You reach it from a step ladder in the middle of the floor.

The bin module

Everything is dimensioned around one footprint: 15 3/8" deep × 11" wide, in 11" and 5 1/2" heights. Three fit across bay B with 7/8" to spare. Two fit across bay A with room left for a small bin beside them. Every tier in the closet clears at least 12 1/4", so a tall bin fits anywhere and two short ones stack in the same slot, which means bins move between bays as the kids change.

Materials and cut list

Two sheets of 3/4" plywood build the whole back wall unit. Shelves are ripped at 15 1/4" and get a pine face strip glued and nailed to the front edge, which hides the plywood edge and stiffens the span, giving a 16" finished depth. Bay B's 33 7/8" shelves get a 1×3 face strip rather than a 1×2, because that is the longest span in the build.

At big-box pricing this comes to roughly $260 to $300 for two sheets of sanded birch plywood, the pine, screws and finish. Bins are on top of that and can be bought a tier at a time.

Why fixed cleats rather than a track system. Wire shelving and adjustable standards sag under bin weight and the wires catch on bin bottoms. Plywood on cleats screwed into studs carries 100 lb a shelf without moving, costs less, and the 15" module removes the reason you would want adjustability in the first place.

Build order

  1. Mark out. Snap a level line at 74 1/4" across the whole back wall and confirm it clears the soffit by 1 3/4" at both ends. Find and mark studs on painter's tape at that height. Mark the bay lines at 27 1/2, 28 1/4, 62 1/8 and 62 7/8 on the floor and the wall.
  2. Electrical. Measure the outlet rough-in from the back corner along the left wall and decide whether to move it forward, as above. Trim it out. Add the second receptacle in bay C at 40" if you need it. Build the vented access panel for the driver pocket on the left end wall now, while the closet is still empty. Confirm the left wall's thickness at the same time, before any cleat goes into it.
  3. Floor. Done. The floating floor is in, and it runs under where the unit goes, which is what this plan assumes. Nothing here is fastened through it, so it stays free to move. Re-measure the ceiling off the finished floor before cutting the full-height divider.
  4. Cut and finish outside the closet. Rip the sheets, cut to length, glue and nail the face strips on, sand, prime and paint. Painting a 36" deep closet with shelves already in it is miserable.
  5. Cleats. Back wall first; it is ordinary wall below 76" and carries the load. Left and right end cleats after, once you have confirmed what the left wall is made of. Two sets of lines. Bay B cleat tops at 14 1/4, 29 1/4, 44 1/4, 59 1/4 and 73 1/2. Bay A cleat tops at 14 1/4, 31 1/4, 46 1/4, 61 1/4 and 73 1/2. Bay C gets one at 71 1/4. Two screws per stud into every cleat, and the left-wall side cleats follow bay A's numbers.
  6. Dividers. Stand both dividers plumb, packed up 1/4" off the floor, and tack them to the back cleats.
  7. Shelves, bottom to top. Drop each onto its cleat, screw down into the cleat, then screw through the dividers into the shelf edges. Check level as you go; the shelves are what hold the dividers straight.
  8. Fit-out. Generator onto the left end of the 15" shelf, cord through the notch, plugged in, and check the display reads from the doorway. Stand the vacuum and broom in bay C. Hooks on the front wall right of the door and on the left face of the laundry chase, low, for backpacks. Shelf-edge labels last, once the bins have found their homes.

Options worth considering

  • Left-wall tower, phase 2. The left end wall has 20 1/2" of unused depth in front of the unit. A 12" deep by 20 1/2" wide stack fits there, out of the door's path, on a third sheet of plywood. Screw its outer panel to the front wall at its front edge and hang the shelves on cleats screwed to the left wall. Good for games, books and bulk paper. Stop it at 75" rather than running it to the ceiling, with five shelves at 15, 30, 45, 60 and 74 1/4. The driver pocket is 15 1/4" wide, and if it starts forward of the back corner rather than hard against it, a full-height tower would run straight into it and bury equipment that has to stay reachable. Stopping at 75" makes that impossible to get wrong, and it lines the tower up with the top of the main unit. Check the outlet too: the tower stands on the same stretch of left wall, so leave its bottom bay open at the back, or cut the outlet into the tower's own side panel rather than burying it.
  • Left end panel, if the left wall turns out to be thin. The base plan lands bay A's shelf ends on cleats screwed to the left wall. If that wall is a shallow cavity behind the recessed bookcase, put a third plywood panel at the left end of bay A instead, 15 1/4" by 75", standing on the floor and screwed to the back wall cleats and to each shelf edge. The left wall then takes nothing heavier than a couple of short stabilising screws. This pushes the build just past two sheets, so buy a third.
  • The space above 74 1/4" stays empty. There is 21 3/4" of open height across bays A and B, and it is tempting. Leave it. The only surfaces up there are the lit nook, which nothing may fasten to, and the tops of two dividers, which cannot carry a 62" span between them. Anything you build there would hang off the nook, and the nook is a light fixture in someone else's room.
  • Chute lid. The opening in the top of the laundry chase is a drop, so leave it open. The flat of the top around it, at 40 7/8", is the natural landing spot for pockets, chargers and library books on the way through.

Still to confirm

  • Your vacuum's parked height and width. Bay C gives it 24 1/2" of width and 72" of height, which covers everything short of a full-size upright with the handle locked up.
  • Stud spacing on the back wall. Bay B's 33 7/8" cleat needs at least two studs. At 16" on centre it catches two or three; at 24" it may catch only one, in which case add a toggle at the loose end.
  • Ceiling height again after the floating floor goes down. The full-height divider is cut at 95 1/2" to allow for it.
  • Whether the door swings out. A 32" door swinging in would sweep the front of bay A.
  • The generator's height with its carry handle raised, if the handle is fixed rather than folding. The tier gives 16 1/4".
  • How far the outlet rough-in sits from the back corner along the left wall. Under 16" and the unit covers it. This is the one measurement that could still send you back to the drywall.
  • The height of the driver pocket's bottom edge, measured off the finished floor. The top shelf lands at 74 1/4" and its cleat stops at 73 1/2". Anything below 74 1/4" clashes, and the fix is to drop bay A's top shelf to 72". This is the tightest clearance in the plan.
  • How far the pocket sits forward of the back corner. It only matters for the phase 2 tower, which is why that is now drawn stopping at 75".
  • The left end wall's build-up: how thick it is between the closet and the recessed bookcase, and whether 2 1/2" screws into it are safe. If not, build the left end panel instead of wall cleats.
