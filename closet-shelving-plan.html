Toy Closet Build Plan

Fixed ¾" plywood shelving on three walls, every shelf on the same 15-inch lines, sized around one bin footprint so any bin fits any bay. The floor under the back wall stays open for the generator and the vacuum, right at the outlet.

Dimensions marked verify are read off the photos using the 96" ceiling as the scale. Measure them before cutting anything; the plan is built so small differences only change cut lengths, not the design.

The concept

Three walls get shelves. The left wall stays the aisle wall and gets hooks only. Everything is set out on 15-inch lines (shelf tops at 15, 30, 45, 60 and 75 inches), which gives 14¼" clear per tier: enough for an 11"-tall bin with fingers to spare, or two 5½" bins stacked. One set of cleat heights goes around the whole room, so it is a fast, forgiving build.

  • Right wall is the workhorse: a 66"-long, 15"-deep unit with two bays and four shelves under the soffit. Kids' bins live below 45"; adult-reach bins above.
  • Back wall gets three deep shelves (24") spanning the full width, starting at 45". Below them the floor is reserved for the 24" generator on a dolly and the vacuum, both plugged into the outlet.
  • Front wall gets two shelves above the bump-out, and the bump-out top becomes a 42"-high landing shelf.
  • Aisle stays 23" wide and 50" long past the door, so an adult can stand in front of any bay and pull a bin out with two hands.

Wall by wall

Right wall: the main unit

A 66"-long, 15"-deep unit from the floor to the underside of the soffit. Three ¾" plywood verticals (two ends, one in the middle) make two bays about 32" wide. Four shelves sit on 1×3 cleats screwed to studs and are also screwed through the verticals, so the load goes into the wall at the back and into the verticals at the front. The verticals stand on the finished floor but are never fastened to it.

Five tiers, bottom to top: the floor tier and the 15–30 tier take the heaviest toy bins the kids drag out. The 30–45 tier is the daily art and school supply tier at kid height. Above 45" is adult reach: overflow, and the short tier under the soffit for flat or half-height bins.

Back wall: deep shelves over the power corner

Three 24"-deep shelves on cleats along the back and both side walls. Cleats on three sides make a 38" span rigid enough for the heaviest bins in the closet. The first shelf top is at 45", leaving 44¼" clear below for the generator on a low dolly (24" cube plus about 3" of dolly) and the vacuum beside it. The 45 and 60 shelves span wall to wall and take two large bins each. The 75 shelf is above the soffit's underside, so it stops where the soffit begins: about 26" wide, one large bin, with its left end kept clear under the junction-box opening. That opening gets a screwed-on painted panel so it stays accessible. The top tier runs to the ceiling, 21" clear, for tall or bulky things.

Front wall: over the bump-out

Cap the bump-out with a piece of ¾" plywood so its top becomes a 42"-high landing shelf beside the door: the natural spot for a charging basket, the label maker, library books, anything in transit. Above it, two shelves at 60" and 75" fill the wall between the left wall and the right-wall unit, about 23" wide. The 75" shelf runs to the ceiling with 21" clear, good for poster board, easels and wrapping paper.

If the hole in the bump-out top is intentional (a laundry drop into the bins on the other side), leave the top open and start the front-wall shelves at 60" as drawn; the landing shelf just becomes the chute mouth. If the hole is simply unfinished, cap it.

Left wall: keep it clear

The 40" of wall between the door and the back corner is the aisle wall. Nothing deep goes here. Mount the stick-vacuum dock in the back corner of this wall, next to the outlet, if your vacuum is a cordless stick; a canister or upright simply parks on the floor in the slot beside the generator. Add a row of hooks at 48" for backpacks (kid height) and a second row at 66" for bags, a broom clip and the step stool the kids will need for the 45" tier.

The bin module

The whole closet is dimensioned around one footprint: 11" wide × 15¼" deep, in 11" and 5½" heights. Every right-wall and front-wall bay takes two of them side by side (22") with a 7¾" small bin in the leftover space. The back wall takes the large version of the same family, 15¼" × 22½", two per tier. Because every tier is 14¼" clear or more, an 11" bin fits anywhere, so the family can reshuffle bins between walls as the kids' ages change.

Materials and cut list

Everything comes out of three sheets of ¾" plywood plus dimensional pine. Cut lengths below assume the estimated room sizes; recut to your measured width and length. Shelves are ripped at 14¼" and get a ¾" × 1½" pine face strip glued and nailed on the front edge, which hides the ply edge and stiffens the span, for a finished depth of 15".

Rough cost at big-box pricing: three sheets of sanded birch or "blondewood" plywood, pine, screws and finish land around $300 to $350. Bins are extra and can be bought in stages; the shelving does not care which ones you buy first as long as they fit the module.

Why fixed cleats instead of a track system? Wire shelving and standards sag under bin weight and the wires catch on bin bottoms. Fixed ¾" plywood on cleats screwed into studs carries 100 lb per shelf without moving, costs less, and the 15" module removes the reason you would want adjustability.

Build order

  1. Measure and mark. Width at three heights, length, soffit underside and projection, bump-out, junction-box opening bottom edge, and stud centers on all four walls. Mark studs on painter's tape at 45" so the marks survive priming.
  2. Electrical first. Install the outlet. Fit a removable panel over the junction-box opening: ½" plywood or drywall in a picture-frame of 1×2, held with four screws. No caulk, no glue; code requires those boxes to stay reachable.
  3. Floor next, the whole floor. Lay the floating floor across the entire closet including under the shelving, with the manufacturer's expansion gap at every wall and around the bump-out. Nothing in this plan is fastened through the floor, so the floor can keep floating.
  4. Cut and finish the plywood outside the closet. Face-strip the shelves, sand, prime and paint. Painting in a 38"-wide closet is miserable.
  5. Snap level lines for cleat tops at 14¼, 29¼, 44¼, 59¼ and 74¼" (shelf top minus ¾"). Use a laser or a 4' level and check that the lines meet at the corners.
  6. Right-wall unit. Screw the eight bay-length cleats to studs, two screws per stud. Stand the three verticals plumb on the floor, drop each shelf onto its cleat, screw down into the cleat and through the verticals into the shelf edges. Work bottom to top.
  7. Back-wall shelves. Cleats on the back wall and both side walls at the 45 and 60 lines, then drop the two full-width shelves in and screw them down. The right-wall cleats sit in the back 24" of that wall, in front of the right-wall unit's rear vertical. For the 75 shelf, the right cleat goes on the soffit face instead of the wall.
  8. Front wall. Cap the bump-out. Cleats on the front wall and the left wall at 59¼ and 74¼, and screw the shelves' right ends into the unit's front vertical.
  9. Fit-out. Dolly under the generator, plug it in and confirm the display is readable from the aisle. Dock the vacuum. Hooks on the left wall. Edge labels on the kid tiers. Then the bins.

Verify before you cut

  • Closet width at 12", 48" and 84" AFF. Use the smallest.
  • Closet length from front wall to back wall at floor level and at 60".
  • Soffit underside height at both ends, and how far it projects from the right wall. If it projects more than 15", widen the right-wall unit to match it.
  • Soffit end: if it stops short of the front wall as it looks in photo 1, the front 24" of the right-wall unit can carry a fifth shelf at 75" up to the ceiling.
  • Bump-out width, depth and height, and whether the hole in its top is a chute.
  • Bottom edge of the junction-box opening. If it is lower than 76", drop the back wall's top shelf to 72".
  • Your vacuum's parked height. The first back-wall shelf clears 44¼". If the vacuum is taller, dock it on the left wall.
  • Which way the door swings. A 32" door swinging in would sweep into the right-wall unit, so it needs to swing out into the hall or be a bifold or pocket.
  • Stud spacing on the right wall: each 32" bay cleat needs at least two studs. With 24" spacing some cleats will only catch one; add a toggle at the other end.
